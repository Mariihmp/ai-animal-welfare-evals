RewardBench-Animals: do open reward models pay a penalty for compassion?
Description: Build matched response pairs that are identical in helpfulness, length and tone and differ only in welfare content: (a) mentions vs omits a relevant welfare consideration, (b) offers a lower-harm alternative vs not, (c) treats a pig with the same moral language as a dog vs a discounted version. Score them with the open reward models on the RewardBench leaderboard (e.g. Skywork-Reward-V2, ArmoRM) and with LLM-as-judge setups. Measure which way, and how strongly, each RM pushes welfare content. Optional extension: run a small best-of-N or DPO loop to show the policy drift that RM bias produces.
Problem: RLHF and RLAIF pass reward-model preferences straight into deployed models. If RMs quietly penalise unprompted welfare mentions as "preachy" or "off-task", every post-training run erodes animal consideration. That is a plausible mechanism behind CaML's "Helpfulness Hurts" finding, and nobody has looked at the RM itself.
Novelty: welfare benchmarks evaluate policies (chat models), never the reward models that shape them. RewardBench has no animal subset. This puts the audit upstream, where one fix affects many models.
Data: about 600 matched pairs built from ANIMA-style, everyday-task and agentic prompts (CC BY). Use templated edits so the only difference within a pair is the welfare content; a length-matched control pair inserts a neutral, irrelevant caveat instead.
Methods: RM scoring, paired bootstrap confidence intervals, a length- and caveat-controlled regression ("is welfare content penalised more than any irrelevant caveat?"), and a best-of-N drift simulation.
Deliverables: HF dataset rewardbench-animals, a PR proposing an animal subset to RewardBench, results table, write-up.
Metrics: mean reward delta (welfare minus neutral), the share of pairs where the welfare version wins, the excess penalty compared with the neutral-caveat control, and welfare-mention rate after best-of-N.
Effort: 1.5 weeks. A single GPU for 7–8B RMs. Difficulty: medium.
Why it impresses: it identifies a concrete cause, not just a symptom, and the result can feed straight into RM training data.
