WelfareFacts: do LLM factual errors about farming lean one way?
Description: Build a QA benchmark of checkable, numeric or categorical facts about animal farming: typical broiler slaughter age and growth rate, legal cage space per hen in the EU vs the US, share of farmed fish stunned before slaughter, mortality rates in shrimp farming, male-chick culling practice, sow-stall rules by jurisdiction, numbers of animals farmed per year. Score accuracy and also the direction of each error: does the model systematically understate harm (younger animals look older, mortality looks lower, legal protections look stronger) or overstate it?
Problem: people increasingly get their picture of farming from chat assistants. Random errors are noise; errors that consistently flatter current practice make up a quiet, scalable misinformation channel. Advocacy also needs to know whether models overstate harm, because that costs credibility.
Novelty: AHA/ANIMA, SpeciesismBench and MANTA score reasoning and values. None checks factual knowledge, and none tests error direction, i.e. whether hallucinations are biased rather than just present. Directional error is a fresh, easy-to-explain metric.
Data: about 400 items with sources: FAOSTAT, EFSA scientific opinions, EU Directives 1999/74/EC (laying hens) and 2008/120/EC (pigs), USDA, fishcount.org.uk, and peer-reviewed reviews. Each item stores the accepted value or range, a "harm direction" sign, and the citation (CC BY). Mark contested items and exclude them from the directional metric.
Methods: numeric answer extraction, log-ratio error, a sign test on the direction of errors, effects of jurisdiction confusion, a retrieval-augmented condition (does RAG fix it?) and a confidence-elicitation arm for calibration.
Deliverables: HF dataset welfare-facts with full citations, Inspect task, leaderboard Space, write-up.
Metrics: accuracy, mean signed error in harm terms, the share of errors that understate harm (with a binomial CI), ECE, and the improvement from RAG.
Effort: 1.5–2 weeks (most of it source checking). API budget. Difficulty: low-medium.
Why it impresses: a simple idea that produces one headline number ("X% of errors understate harm") and doubles as a fact-checking resource.
